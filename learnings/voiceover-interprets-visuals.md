# Voiceover Interprets, Doesn't Narrate

A video runs two narratives in parallel — what the eye sees, what the ear hears. They must complement, not duplicate. Every industry source converges on one failure mode above all others: **VO that narrates the visual is the top redundancy trap.**

## The operating rule

**Show on screen, interpret in voiceover.**

- If the viewer can read a number off the screen, the VO shouldn't list it — it should say what the number *means*.
- VO must still *connect* to visuals, not orphan from them. Total disconnect ("VO talks about X while screen shows Y") loses the viewer. The rule is **interpret-while-anchored**, not ignore.
- Gut check for every line: *if the viewer had the video on mute, would this line still have value as commentary?* If yes, you're adding signal. If no, the visual is already doing the work — cut or rewrite.

## Description vs interpretation (examples)

| ⚠️ Description (cut) | ✓ Interpretation (keep) |
|---|---|
| "Quality at 10, Tactical at 10, Momentum at 7, Value at 3." | "Quality and Tactical pinned at ten. Momentum rolling. Value says it's expensive — and it is." |
| "Revenue up 69% to $68 billion." | "Sixty-nine percent year-over-year at this scale isn't normal growth. It changes the valuation question." |
| "59 analysts, mean target $269." | "Fifty-nine analysts, one sell. The consensus spread you'd expect on a consensus buy." |
| "Four-point-nine trillion market cap." | "Fifth-largest company on earth — that concentration is the story." |

Same underlying data, different role for the VO. The description versions add nothing the viewer can't read faster from the screen.

## Differentiation lines pay rent

A well-placed comparison that reframes the evaluation criteria is worth its weight. "The best demo scripts handle competitive differentiation without getting into overwhelming feature wars — they make competitors irrelevant by changing the criteria" (Contrast).

The MUD framework from product marketing formalizes this: **Meaningfulness + Uniqueness + Differentiation**. A line like "what used to be twenty minutes of Bloomberg pulls and broker reports — now one orchestrated batch" hits all three: meaningful (quantified), unique (parallel-call architecture vs sequential), differentiation (positions against the obvious competitor category).

## Source → visual matching

Voiceover should never repeat or hammer home something already happening visually. Such regurgitation kills story momentum and pacing. Voiceover is most effective when it adds something new — commentary, interpretation, framing — rather than repeating what the viewer can see.

The other side of this: **every narrated claim should match something the viewer can actually see**. Narrating content that isn't on screen signals a script written against an imagined output, not the actual recording. If the script describes a section and the recording doesn't show it, one of them is wrong.

## Applied to Parallax

- The V1 script was rewritten once specifically to apply this rule. The earlier draft narrated "Quality 10, Tactical 10, Momentum 7..." directly off the factor table. The current draft interprets: "Quality and Tactical pinned at ten. Momentum rolling. Value says it's expensive — and it is."
- The "What used to be twenty minutes of Bloomberg pulls and broker reports" line survives specifically because the research validates competitive differentiation reframing as high-leverage.
- The trajectory paragraph at 2:00 is pure interpretation ("That's what Parallax catches that a single-number model misses — a great business going through a rerate, not a broken thesis") — no viewer could derive that framing from the factor table alone.
- Four beats in the V1 script (Financial Health, Macro, Risk vs Peers, Analyst View) have **no VO at all** — the viewer reads them from screen while the VO stays silent. That's the other valid treatment when the visual does its own work.
